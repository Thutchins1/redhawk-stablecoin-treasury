TEAM PROJECT BRIEF
Team name: Tanner Hutchins (solo)
Challenge number: 28 — Stablecoin treasury risk monitor
User and job role: Treasury analyst at a small company that keeps part of its operating cash in stablecoins (USDC, USDT, DAI).
Pain point: During the March 2023 USDC depeg, treasury teams had no quick way to see how far off $1.00 they were, how exposed they were to one issuer, or whether their "sell if it breaks" instinct would actually help. Decisions were made on a weekend from exchange screenshots.
One workflow we will improve: Daily treasury risk check. Analyst opens one page, sees peg deviation and issuer concentration against the firm's written policy, reads plain-language alerts, and can replay a past depeg against that policy before deciding whether the policy is sane.
Inputs and data sources: Fictional treasury fixture (data/stablecoin_treasury.json, USD 250,000 across three stablecoins, explicitly synthetic). Stablecoin prices from CoinGecko via its MCP connector, cached in data/stablecoin_prices.json with source and retrieval date: current snapshot (2026-11-06) and daily 00:00 UTC snapshots for 2023-03-09 to 2023-03-14.
Output or decision supported: Whether the treasury is inside policy today, which issuer or asset is the problem, and whether the stated rebalance rule would have helped or hurt in March 2023. The tool informs; a human decides. No trades are executed.
Three features in scope:
 1. Peg deviation scoring per asset in basis points with ok / warning / critical levels and source-cited alerts (MVP 2).
 2. Issuer concentration: share per issuer, Herfindahl index, effective-issuer count, cap breach alert (MVP 2).
 3. March 2023 depeg replay: hold path versus policy path day by day, every forced sale logged with its shortfall versus par, and a one-sentence verdict (MVP 3 stretch).
What we will leave out: Live exchange execution, wallet connections, DeFiLlama reserve data, slippage and fee modelling, multi-currency treasuries, authentication (demo routes are unauthenticated and local-only).
Demo acceptance criteria:
 - Valid input: GET /api/treasury on the fixture returns par value 250,000.00, holding values that sum to the total to the cent, one issuer-concentration alert (Circle above 50% cap) and no peg alerts.
 - Missing/invalid input: a holding with no price, repeated assets, more than six decimals, or a warning threshold above the critical threshold is rejected with a 400 and a specific message, never a guess.
 - Reconciliation: replay hold-path value on 2023-03-14 equals a direct valuation at that day's prices; policy path units equal original minus sold; the policy ends behind holding and the realized shortfall is positive.
 All three are automated in tests/stablecoin.test.js (npm test, 27 passing: 18 from the starter pack plus 9 for this challenge).
Tools and integrations: Node 22+ native HTTP backend from the starter pack, lib/finance.js BigInt money utilities, CoinGecko MCP connector (free, no key) for price retrieval, Claude Code as the build assistant. Supabase team model available but not required for the offline demo.
Source and permission notes: CoinGecko data used under its free public terms via the official MCP connector; retrieval dates recorded in data/stablecoin_prices.json. Treasury holdings and policy thresholds are invented for the demo and labelled synthetic.
Known limitations: Six daily price points for the replay (no intraday lows, which were worse). Rule executes at the snapshot price with no slippage, fees or settlement delay. Thresholds are team assumptions, not regulatory guidance. The summary text is a deterministic template; no language model generates figures. Not a compliance or production tool.

DEMO PROCEDURE (about two minutes)
1. npm run dev, open http://127.0.0.1:4180/
2. Point at the KPI row: treasury is $0.xx under par today, Circle is 60% of the book, one medium alert.
3. Scroll to Policy, change "Act at" from 200 to 100 bps, click Re-score. Show alerts and replay update from the same engine.
4. Scroll to the replay chart: both lines together until March 12, policy line drops and never recovers. Read the verdict sentence.
5. Click Generate summary: every number traces back to the table above it.
6. Show npm test output: 27 passing, including the three acceptance checks.
