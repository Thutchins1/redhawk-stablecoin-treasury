PITCH CHEAT SHEET — Challenge 28, Stablecoin Treasury Monitor
Read this three times. Then practice the two-minute script out loud twice.

------------------------------------------------------------
FIVE THINGS TO KNOW COLD
------------------------------------------------------------
1. Stablecoin = a crypto token meant to always be worth $1.00. Companies hold them like cash. USDC, USDT, DAI are the big three.
2. Peg deviation = how far the price is from $1.00. We measure it in basis points (bps). 100 bps = 1%. A 2% drop is 200 bps.
3. Issuer concentration = how much of the money depends on one company. Circle issues USDC, Tether issues USDT, MakerDAO issues DAI. If 60% of your cash is USDC and Circle has a problem, 60% of your cash has a problem.
4. March 2023 = the one time USDC broke. Silicon Valley Bank failed, Circle had $3.3 billion stuck there, USDC fell to 96 cents over a weekend, then recovered by Tuesday.
5. The replay = we take the real prices from that week and ask "if the company had a rule that said sell when it drops 2%, what would have happened?" Answer: they'd have sold at the bottom and lost about $3,300 that holding would have gotten back.

------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTIONS JUDGES WILL PROBABLY ASK
------------------------------------------------------------
Q: Who is the user?
A: A treasury analyst or finance manager at a company that holds some operating cash in stablecoins.

Q: Where does the data come from?
A: CoinGecko, through its official connector. Current prices and daily snapshots from March 2023. Dates and sources are recorded in the data file. The treasury holdings are fictional, the pack requires that.

Q: What did you use AI for?
A: Claude Code built the code with me directing it, which is how the starter pack told us to work. I picked the challenge and the replay angle and tested the policy settings. The app itself does not use AI for any number; the pack says calculations must be in code.

Q: What are the limitations?
A: Six daily price points, so it misses intraday lows which were worse. No fees or slippage. Thresholds are our assumptions. Not a compliance tool.

Q: What would you add next?
A: More crash scenarios beyond the two. Live price refresh. Hooking it to the Supabase team database so a whole finance team shares it.

Q: Why does the policy lose money? Isn't selling the safe move?
A: Selling locks in the loss. Holding only loses if the coin never recovers. USDC recovered in three days because Circle got its money back when the government backstopped SVB deposits. The tool's point is that you should test the rule against history before trusting it.

Q: Why only three stablecoins?
A: They are the three biggest and the ones a real treasury would hold. The engine accepts any list.
